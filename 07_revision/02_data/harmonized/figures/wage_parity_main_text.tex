Figure \ref{fig:gender_norms} relates both gaps to wage parity, defined as one minus the reported gender wage gap, with both panels on the same female-minus-male percentage-point scale. Women's representation among high achievers rises with wage parity (panel a): a 0.1 increase is associated with about 10 percentage points in the pipeline gap in women's favor. The choice gap is comparatively flat (panel b): the same increase is associated with about 2.3 percentage points in men's favor, a slope not statistically different from zero that reverses sign when Uganda is excluded.\footnote{Online \ref{sec:appendix_wage_parity} describes the differing wage concepts, coverage and years and reports sensitivity checks on both scales.} These are descriptive correlations across ten settings, not effects of wage parity on either gap.
